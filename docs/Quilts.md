## The Renee Fields Quilt Project

Renee passed away almost one year ago and she is deeply missed.  She was a talented quilter, crafter and artist.  Her designs were often whimsical and humorous and full of stories. She worked in many mediums and didn't seemed married to any particular style.  I think she liked learning new things and trying out different techniques.  In all her work she had incredible attention to detail and a rich sense of color.

My hope is that we can take some of her unfinished quilts and finish them in a way that honors her memory.  

![halfwidth](https://gist.githubusercontent.com/wildthinkslaboratory/ac98c0bb68ccf7528dc39fa1922d2bdb/raw/e5112505b2ca545b80f5fb2a1cfc09f58ca165e9/quilt.JPG)

The first quilt is underway and is intended as a wedding present for her nephew Isaiah.  I intended that project to be a collaborative project and in fact I've had much help from Peter, my friend Aura and my sons Izzy and Andre.  Unfortunately, because of the time crunch caused by Isaiah's wedding date in May, I've just had to buckle down and get it done.  As a result I haven't been able to include more people in the design and construction process.  In fact there was little time for design at all and I think we've gotten lucky that the design seems to be working out.  However, I hope that the next quilt can involve more people.  So I've made this web page for us to share ideas and inspirations for the next quilt.  This is a private (hidden) web page, but if I've shared this link with you it's an invitation to share your ideas and comments about the next quilt design.  You can email me pictures, comments, opinions, ideas, links to relevant web pages and I'll add them to the page so others can see.  Of course, these quilts will likely end up as presents for family members (that's up to Peter) so please be aware and don't share this page with anyone who might be the intended recipient!

For the next quilt I'm considering tackling this beautiful blue shell piece.  Many of the fabrics have whimsical prints including:

- statue of liberty
- chanukah prints, menorahs, jewish stars
- Renee's signature betty boop print
- animals: pteredactls, cats, dogs, cows, lambs, chickens
- micky mouse
- fireworks, stars
- fish and under water scenes. 
- a mandala print
- japanese style prints.  
- yankees baseballs
- ships and airplanes


It's around 40 by 56 inches.  The color scheme is really lovely.  It's main color theme is a soft blue with a hint of gray.  All the other colors fluctuate around that main color, varying the darkness and amount of gray.  There are flecks of bright orange, yellow and red spattered around, but only minimally.  

I dramatically changed the color scheme of the Renee quilt for Isaiah.  I think that would be a mistake for this quilt since the color scheme is such a defining feature.  The other defining feature is the soft rounded shapes of the shells. 

This quilt is hand pieced.  I was initially concerned because I'm not skilled at and don't have the patience for hand quilting.  The women at the quilt shop said that was silly and that of course a hand pieced item could be creatively machine quilted.  

## Unanswered Design Questions
- What size should this quilt be?  It could be extended to a Queen/double size.  Queens make nice presents for newlyweds since they are perfect for snuggling under.  Underside can be made of flannel.  It would make a lovely baby quilt but I'm not sure if anyone is having a baby soon.  I firmly believe that baby quilts should tolerate lots of washing so not sure if hand pieced and hand quilted would qualify.  Hand pieced, machine quilted probably would.

- If we extend this quilt to a queen/double how do we maintain the quilts properties or create an addition that has synergy with it? The color scheme and choice of prints would be easy to extend by I'm concerned about the shapes.  I'm not a hugely experienced quilter and I hope to include some beginning quilters.  That often means squares, triangles and straight line designs.  Is there a way to bring softness and roundness to a border that isn't too hard to implement?  Appliques might be an easyish way to bring some rounded lines to any addition.
